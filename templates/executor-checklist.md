# Executor / Estate Trustee Checklist — Canada

> Use this as a working checklist when settling a Canadian estate. Timing varies; this is a typical sequence. Engage a lawyer and accountant unless the estate is very simple.

## First week

- [ ] Locate the original will and any codicils
- [ ] Locate the deceased's powers of attorney (no longer in force, but useful for records)
- [ ] Make funeral arrangements per the deceased's wishes (cost is recoverable from the estate)
- [ ] Obtain 10+ certified copies of the death certificate from the funeral home or Vital Statistics
- [ ] Secure the deceased's home, vehicles, valuables
- [ ] Notify family, employer, executor's lawyer

## First month

- [ ] Apply for the CPP Death Benefit ($2,500) at Service Canada
- [ ] Apply for CPP Survivor's Pension (if eligible spouse) and Children's Benefit
- [ ] Notify all banks, brokerages, insurance companies, pension plans
- [ ] Identify and inventory all assets (real estate, vehicles, accounts, investments, RRSP/RRIF/TFSA, life insurance, personal property)
- [ ] Identify and list all liabilities (mortgages, credit cards, loans, taxes owing)
- [ ] Cancel: provincial health card, driver's licence, passport, OAS/CPP (further benefits), credit cards, subscriptions
- [ ] Redirect mail (Canada Post)
- [ ] Open an estate bank account ("Estate of [DECEASED NAME]")

## First 3 months

- [ ] Apply for **probate** (Certificate of Appointment of Estate Trustee in Ontario; Grant of Probate elsewhere) — pay the Estate Administration Tax (ON ~1.5% over $50K)
- [ ] If real estate is held jointly with right of survivorship: transfer title using a Survivorship Application
- [ ] If solely owned: transfer requires probate
- [ ] Pay urgent debts and ongoing expenses (mortgage, utilities, insurance)

## First year

- [ ] File the deceased's **final T1 return** by April 30 of the year after death (or 6 months after death if later)
- [ ] Consider filing optional **Rights or Things return** (T1) and **Income from a Testamentary Trust return**
- [ ] If the estate has income during administration, file a **T3 Trust return** annually
- [ ] Request a **Clearance Certificate** from CRA confirming all taxes paid before final distribution
- [ ] Distribute specific bequests once probate granted and debts paid
- [ ] Account to beneficiaries (formal "passing of accounts" if estate is contentious or under court supervision)

## Tax considerations

- **Deemed disposition**: All capital property is deemed disposed at fair market value on the date of death, except property rolling over to a surviving spouse or spousal trust.
- **RRSP/RRIF**: Full balance taxable on the final T1 unless rolled to a spouse, financially dependent child, or grandchild with infirmity.
- **TFSA**: Tax-free to estate; can transfer to spouse's TFSA via "successor holder" designation.
- **Principal residence exemption**: Applies to one residence per family unit per year.
- **Capital losses** realized after death can be carried back to the final T1.

## When to engage professionals

- **Lawyer**: Probate application, contested estates, complex real-estate transfers, US-situs property
- **Accountant**: Final T1, T3 trust returns, post-mortem tax planning (CDA strip, loss carryback)
- **Financial advisor**: Investment-account transfers, beneficiary administration

## Compensation

- Executor compensation in most provinces is around 2.5% on receipts + 2.5% on disbursements, subject to court approval. Document time and expenses meticulously.
